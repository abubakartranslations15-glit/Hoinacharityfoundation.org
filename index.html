Why we exist

You cannot teach a hungry child, and you cannot rebuild a frightened community

Before a child can learn, their stomach needs to be full. Before a family can plan for tomorrow, their street needs to be safe. HOINA works on the problems that come first — because that is where real change has to start.

Our most urgent work

A generation with no future ambition becomes a generation the streets raise instead

Idle, hopeless young people are being pulled into phone-snatching, armed robbery, and violence — often against women, and often fatal for tricycle and motorcycle riders who never see it coming. Some of this is manipulated by people with an agenda. Underneath almost all of it is poverty and a total lack of direction. Alongside this, long-running farmer-herder clashes continue to cost lives every year. HOINA runs community dialogues, peace clubs, and security-awareness campaigns — and stands as legal representation for survivors of gender-based violence, at a time when victims are too often blamed instead of protected.

Hunger & nutrition

The most humiliating thing a person can do is beg for their next meal

With food prices out of reach for even middle-income households, we are seeing more children and pregnant women going without proper nutrition than at any point in recent memory. Dignity starts with a full stomach — not a lecture about the future. HOINA partners with agencies to distribute nutritious food and essential supplies directly to the households that need it most, prioritizing pregnant women, nursing mothers, and young children.

Food Distribution Child Nutrition Family Relief
Maternal & child health

Some mothers are one or two hours from the nearest clinic when labour begins

A poorly-fed pregnant woman often lacks the strength for a safe delivery, and cesarean fees can be out of reach for families already struggling to eat. Nigeria's NIN-linked scheme already covers free cesarean sections in government hospitals — but that information rarely reaches rural communities, and it is villagers, not city residents, who need it most. HOINA works to close that awareness gap and connect families to the maternal support they're entitled to but don't know exists.

Maternal Health Rural Access Awareness Campaigns
Education, once the basics are met

A child in a torn uniform, unsure if there's food at home, still deserves to learn

Many children in government schools carry more worry than a child should — no guarantee of a meal after class, no new uniform, no supplies. HOINA distributes books, stationery and learning materials to the children who need them most, and trains teachers in counseling so they can recognize what a struggling child is carrying, and respond with patience instead of punishment.
